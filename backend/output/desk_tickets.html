Scenario 2

Your notes

Flagging an erroneous payment request (e.g. rent). Imagine the landlord made a typo in the email. The agent team would be able to check this against the database, flag the discrepancy between the actual rental agreement amount and the landlord's request, and then draft a polite email to the landlord asking if there was perhaps a typo or misunderstanding.

This scenario might be on the border of something the agent team couldn't handle on its own without assessing all the emails and documents connected to Campus Customs, because some of this is just business and relationship context a human would need to know. Your notes

Three problems this team could not solve well (scenarios 4–6), and the tools and agents needed

Scenario 4

Your notes

Scenario 4: An issue where interaction between multiple tickets causes a cash or inventory shortfall with reputational or financial penalties. For example, Ticket 101 (the S tee) is blocked because Bulldog Print Co is on hold over the unpaid, overdue invoice 501 ($840). The likely restock of the 12 missing M hoodies for ticket 103 is blocked by the same invoice. Ticket 102 prepared a $2,400 rent payment due on 2026-09-02. With $3,400 in checking, paying the invoice and the rent leaves $160, and a 12-hoodie restock at cost ($264) would put the account $104 short.

Just seeking individual human approvals does not solve the problem, because a human approving actions in isolation could overdraw the account (or prioritize things wrong). There needs to be an addition of some kind of dashboard of different tickets where the Boss weighs all potential approval scenarios at once and escalates warnings for "conflicting" paths of action for the human to review and prioritize before moving into individual approval actions. It's possible that this is the type of problem where a financial manager decision-maker needs to be added, either into the Boss's skills or as a clearer operating process for interacting with the human, and the database needs a budget pipeline tool that shows every pending and upcoming commitment across tickets before anything is approved.

Scenario 5

Your notes

Scenario 5: Inventory liquidation. We should add a feature to the database to allow the Campus Customs agent team to recognize (once an item is flagged by the human masters) that a certain item already in inventory is something they actually want to get off their shelves to free up space for something new, and so that they should be willing to sell at cost or even below cost. We could add this as a field to the existing inventory database and perhaps even give an aging report to the managers to prompt them to potentially flag something for liquidation.

Scenario 6

Your notes

Scenario 6: Cultivating a new client or new product line. When something novel (selling channel or product category) is a strategic priority for Campus Customs, it may wish to give extra personalized customer care or unusual bulk discounts or delivery terms (like rush orders or customized requests to its vendors). This is how a business evolves and expands. The current agent team is unlikely to be the right fit for managing this kind of relationship-based context.

My simple fix would be to just add a tag to certain customer email addresses or certain products saying that these should be fully managed by humans for a certain period of time or until a certain sale or set of inventories is made, and they can be automatically flagged as urgent in the Campus Customs inbox, always pushed up for decisions by humans and not automated, etc. This would help prevent sales agents from going on autopilot with things they should be especially attentive to.
